# Funding and sponsorship

## How it's funded today

The portfolio is **self-funded by anonymous individuals and organizations** who
want this infrastructure to exist. There is no per-seat licensing, no platform
pays for its own copy, and nothing is gated behind a paywall — it is built as a
public good and given away. Sponsorship is welcome but not assumed; the work
continues either way.

## Why sponsorship, not seats

The natural beneficiary of this work is not a single platform — it's the long
tail of platforms that all need the same plumbing and all rebuild it badly.
Per-seat or per-tool licensing would fragment that user base and create the wrong
incentive (gate features behind a paywall). So where the work is sponsored, the
right shape is restricted/grant capital or infrastructure underwriting aligned
with the public-goods nature of the portfolio — not licensing.

## Ways to sponsor

If you would like to help sustain or expand the work, these are the paths that
fit, in rough priority:

1. **Child-safety / internet-integrity grants** — e.g. the Tech Coalition's
   Lantern program, or its Safe Online / End Violence Fund backers. A restricted
   grant for maintainer time plus the NCMEC vector-validation relationship that
   makes HashKit's conformance suite load-bearing.
2. **Infrastructure sponsor** — the Cloudflare CSAM scanner is free for the same
   reason the portfolio is free. Underwriting the open hashing core extends that
   same "raise the floor" mission to platforms not behind a given proxy.
3. **Per-tool support** — for platforms that need a signed conformance
   attestation ("verified against vector set vX, NCMEC-cross-checked") or
   prioritized maintenance. Never a paywall on the core.

## The pitch (to a potential foundation funder)

Every Lantern-member platform independently re-audits its own PDQ port, rebuilds
the same NCMEC submit-upload-finish state machine from a PDF, and improvises
evidence retention in raw S3 buckets — paying ten times over for plumbing that
should be a shared, verified public good. Fund one maintainer plus, critically,
the NCMEC vector-validation relationship, and the whole membership gets a
byte-identical hashing primitive with cross-checked conformance vectors, a
maintained CyberTipline filing library, and statutory-timer evidence custody —
cheaper for the coalition than any single member's re-audit. The deliverable is
concrete and already half-built: digitalharm.org's Chapter 06 maps this exact
detection landscape, and this portfolio is the buildable layer beneath it,
lowering the floor so the long tail of 20-person UGC platforms can report to
NCMEC at all.

## What sponsorship would support

- A maintainer's time
- Outside counsel on retainer for the legal-tier tools (cybertip-cli, evidencevault)
- The NCMEC ESP relationship and the validation work that produces the cross-checked vector suite
- CI infrastructure, hosting for hashstream's reference deployment, and ancillary OSS overhead

## What sponsors get

- A logo slot on the [/tools page](https://digitalharm.org/tools) and per-package READMEs (tasteful, not noisy)
- Recognition in release notes
- Quarterly impact reports (downloads, adopting platforms, CyberTipline reports filed by users of the libraries — to the extent these can be measured ethically)
- A public commitment to the safety policy and the threat model. We do not accept sponsorship from organizations that materially profit from the harms this portfolio addresses.
