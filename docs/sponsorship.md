# Funding and sponsorship

## The pitch

Lead sponsor: the Tech Coalition's Lantern program (or its Safe Online / End Violence Fund backers). Pitch: every Lantern member platform independently re-audits its own PDQ port, rebuilds the same NCMEC submit-upload-finish state machine from a PDF, and improvises evidence retention in raw S3 buckets — paying ten times over for plumbing that should be a shared, verified public good. Fund one maintainer plus, critically, the NCMEC vector-validation relationship, and the whole membership gets a byte-identical hashing primitive with cross-checked conformance vectors, a maintained CyberTipline filing library, and statutory-timer evidence custody — cheaper for the coalition than any single member's re-audit. The deliverable they can point to is concrete and already half-built: digitalharm.org's Chapter 06 maps this exact detection landscape; this portfolio is the buildable layer beneath it, lowering the floor so the long tail of 20-person UGC platforms can report to NCMEC at all. A Cloudflare-style infrastructure sponsor is the natural secondary — they already give the CSAM scanner away free to raise the baseline; underwriting the open hashing core extends that same "raise the floor" mission to platforms not behind their proxy.

## Why grant capital, not seats

The buyer for this work is not a single platform — it's the long tail of platforms that all need the same plumbing and all rebuild it badly. Per-seat or per-tool licensing fragments the user base and creates the wrong incentive (gate features behind a paywall). Restricted/grant capital from child-safety foundations, public funders, and infrastructure sponsors aligns the maintenance budget with the public-goods nature of the portfolio.

## Funding asks

Three concrete ways to fund the work, in priority order:

1. **Tech Coalition Lantern / Safe Online / End Violence Fund** — restricted grant for one maintainer FTE plus the NCMEC vector-validation relationship that makes HashKit's conformance suite load-bearing.
2. **Cloudflare-style infrastructure sponsor** — the Cloudflare CSAM scanner is free for the same reason the portfolio is free. A natural secondary that gets to claim it raised the floor for non-Cloudflare platforms too.
3. **Per-tool commercial support tier** — for platforms that need a signed conformance attestation ("verified against vector set vX, NCMEC-cross-checked") or prioritized maintenance, not a paywall.

## What sponsors are funding

- A solo maintainer's time, full-time, for the first 12 months
- Outside counsel on retainer for the legal-tier tools (cybertip-cli, evidencevault)
- The NCMEC ESP relationship and the validation work that produces the cross-checked vector suite
- CI infrastructure, hosting for hashstream's reference deployment, and ancillary OSS overhead

## What sponsors get

- A logo slot on the [/tools page](https://digitalharm.org/tools) and per-package READMEs (designed to be tasteful, not noisy)
- Recognition in release notes
- Quarterly impact reports (downloads, adopting platforms, CyberTipline reports filed by users of the libraries — to the extent these can be measured ethically)
- A public commitment to the safety policy and the threat model. We do not accept sponsorship from organizations that materially profit from the harms this portfolio addresses.
